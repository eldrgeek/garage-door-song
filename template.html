Chapter one

Wednesday night on the Hootnet

The Hootnet is a video call that meets every Wednesday. On this Wednesday the crew was:

  • Jess
  • Harold
  • Noel
  • George
  • Mike

The talk wandered, the way it does. Then someone said this:

“What do young people do when they want to feel their own autonomy? They form a band. And they make music their parents don’t understand.”

Someone else said that sounded like a song. Mike agreed, and he handed the line to his AI team to produce.

{{S1}}
Chapter two

The team takes the line

The song went from one sentence to a finished track in a single evening, in four steps.

  1. First draft: a rock songClaude wrote a garage-rock song. It had a twist in the bridge: Dad walks out to the driveway, and instead of yelling he says he had a band in 1979.
  2. Mike: “Turn it into a rap with tight lyrics”Claude rebuilt it as a rap. Verses one and two have 16 bars each. The hook is short enough to chant. Verse three drops the beat so Dad’s confession lands, and the final hook changes one line: “You used to make this noise too.”
  3. Mike: “Use your Suno skills to make this one”Yeshie, the fleet’s browser hands, drove Suno in Mike’s own Chrome. It filled in the lyrics, the style prompt and the title, then pressed Create. Suno produced two takes.
  4. The pickMike listened and chose Take 1. His verdict was that it was a banger.

Then Mike asked for this page, with pictures to match the song. He asked the team to involve as many AIs as possible, and to credit the people who wrote the image prompts and the tools that made the images.

Chapter three

Seven AIs, ten pictures

Claude broke the song into ten scenes and wrote one shared brief. The brief fixed the cast, so Jenny, Sam, the drummer, Mom and Dad look like the same people from picture to picture. It also fixed the look: a warm bulb, a rolled-up garage door, gritty but hopeful. It banned real logos and band names.

Four AIs wrote the prompts: Claude, GPT, Gemini and Grok. Three different AI image makers rendered them: Grok Imagine, OpenAI’s GPT Image and Google’s Nano Banana. No prompt writer was paired with only one image maker. The table at the bottom of the page shows which pair made each picture.
